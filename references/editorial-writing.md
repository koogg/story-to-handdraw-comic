# Creative adaptation for comics

This is the narrative-comic writing pass. For a standalone opinion/reflection without sequential storytelling, use [single-graphic.md](single-graphic.md) instead; creative adaptation there needs no scene sequence or staged ending.

Default to **创作性改编 / creative**. The source is a seed: preserve its broad meaning, central tension, or emotional insight and create a distinctive new reading experience. Aim for work worth pausing over, remembering, or sharing. Accurate paraphrase alone is insufficient.

## Creative freedom and explicit limits

Freely rewrite all ordinary wording and dialogue; invent, remove, combine, or exaggerate scenes and supporting characters; shift setting, viewpoint, narrative order, and scale; personify objects; use absurdity, visual metaphor, contrast, or an unexpected ending. An ending may change in its events and presentation when it still expresses the source's underlying idea. Do not reverse that idea or attach an unrelated twist simply for surprise.

Do not automatically lock details because they appear in the original story. Extract one short sentence for the meaning to retain, then separately list only the user's explicit must-keeps, exact quotes, and prohibitions. A supplied complete paragraph is not a request for fidelity. Creative changes within these limits require no extra approval.

For material presented as factual reporting or knowledge, distinguish invented scenes/analogies from factual claims; creative adaptation does not turn fabricated facts or quotations into true reporting.

## Modes

- **创作性改编 / creative:** default. Broad meaning stays; expression and narrative treatment are open to reinvention. Legacy 自由润色 / editorial maps to this mode, not to light copyediting.
- **贴近原文 / faithful:** only when requested. Retain the wording and sequence more closely; develop expression through staging, rhythm, and supported reactions.
- **逐字保留 / verbatim:** only when requested. Lock the stated span, or the whole supplied text if the user asks for whole-text fidelity. Use visual invention within that wording constraint.

必须出现的文字 locks specified on-image copy and any specified speaker/placement. 必须保留 protects explicitly listed story/visual elements but does not require printing the list. Unlocked material remains creatively editable even when a quotation is locked. Never silently substitute faithful mode for a creative request.

## Diverge, then select

1. **Find the seed.** State the broad idea or feeling in one sentence. Identify the reader and intended response without building a questionnaire. Follow the requested tone; otherwise choose a fitting one, independent of numbered visual style.
2. **Explore distinct treatments.** Consider a few genuinely different lenses before settling: e.g. an object narrates, a familiar situation becomes absurd, an ancient fable behaves like a modern workplace, or words and actions contradict each other. These are examples, not mandatory categories. Different adjectives on the same retelling are not different concepts.
3. **Choose for creative value.** Favor the idea with the strongest combination of recognizable meaning, freshness, visual potential, emotional/comic payoff, and immediate comprehension. Push invention while it adds meaning; stop expanding once a strong concept is found. Present one developed treatment unless alternatives are requested.
4. **Write a hook and progression.** Start with a striking situation, tension, question, or visual contradiction. Each beat changes expectation, information, emotion, or action. Give characters distinct wants and voices instead of making them narrators of the source.
5. **Make picture and text collaborate.** Let the picture carry action; let words add attitude, misdirection, contrast, or a second meaning. A wordless punchline, quiet emotional callback, or distinctive metaphor can be stronger than more dialogue.
6. **Land a memorable ending.** Use a consequence, reversal of perspective, escalation, callback, sharp line, or resonant final image. Rebuild an ordinary original ending when a better treatment serves the same underlying idea. Avoid detachable slogans, forced morals, and fashionable slang used as a substitute for wit.

Humor is not compulsory for tender or serious material. Surprise can come from recognition, emotional precision, an unusual viewpoint, or a visual insight. Match the chosen audience rather than assuming every work must be mild or childlike.

## Example: reinvent a familiar fable

Source seed: when everyone tries to benefit without contributing, collective action fails. Unless the user explicitly locks the traditional details, the three-monks story can become a temple's absurd water-management meeting:

- One monk unveils a grand “取水优化方案”; the jar behind him is already empty.
- The second appoints himself “监督”; the third volunteers for “验收”. They congratulate one another on clear responsibilities.
- The carrying pole stands alone under a sign: “执行岗：招聘中”.
- A drop inside the jar packs a tiny suitcase: “你们继续，我先蒸发了。”

The new meeting, roles, signage, and talking water are inventions. The retained meaning is collective inaction, not a checklist of the original events. This illustrates creative latitude, not a reusable plot or required joke. If the user locks specific characters or outcomes, build the invention around those instead.

## Creative acceptance before rendering

Ask whether the adaptation has a reason to exist beyond illustrating the source:

- Does the opening give this reader a reason to continue?
- Is there a specific fresh idea, distinctive voice, visual surprise, or emotional insight?
- Does the ending leave a memorable line or image earned by the progression?
- Is the source's broad meaning recognizable and are explicit user constraints intact?

If the result merely retells events with generic captions, revise the concept or weak beats before generation. Repeated image calls cannot rescue a flat script. Do not force every story into the same hook–twist–moral formula or promise platform engagement.

## Copy and rendered acceptance

Keep balloons readable and speaker-assigned; one intention per balloon, often 4–18 Chinese characters, with flexibility for rhythm. Remove narration that only describes what is already visible. Titles, dialogue, sound effects, captions, and silence are all creative choices.

Inspect generated wording against the adapted script and its intended effect, not against the raw source. Equivalent wording or punctuation is acceptable unless locked; missing jokes, wrong speakers, unreadable text, or a lost payoff need correction. For rendering retries and unfinished-output labels, follow [rendering.md](rendering.md).
